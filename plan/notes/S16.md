# S16 — giving the pipelines and the buffers their own modules

Research behind the split that took the pipeline state and the buffer wrappers out of
`crates/render/src/lib.rs`. It stays after the commit because "what stayed behind, and why" is the
part S17 inherits; the outcome is in `plan/BACKLOG.md`.

## What was measured

- `crates/render/src/lib.rs` was 1445 production lines and 434 test lines (F5). One file held the
  renderer (`ViewportRenderer`, `upload`, `render_views`, the camera uniform), eleven pipeline and
  depth-state items, the buffer wrappers and every vertex builder — B4 is crossed at 1000.
- The pipeline block (lines 787–1029 before the move) had no caller outside `ViewportRenderer::new`
  and the test module: `create_triangle_pipeline` twice, `create_line_pipeline` three times,
  `create_shadow_pipeline` once, and four `*_depth_state` builders plus `face_target` that only
  those constructors call.
- The buffer wrappers (`GpuMesh`, `GpuLineList`, `GpuTriangles`) and their two upload helpers
  (`line_list`, `triangle_list`) plus `clamp_size` were read only by `new`, `resize`, `upload` and
  `render_views` in the same file.
- The test module is one `mod tests` at the crate root using `super::*`; it asserts the depth,
  blend and stencil promises of four styles directly on `*_depth_state()` and `FACE_DEPTH_BIAS`,
  which is the contract `pipelines.rs` now states.

## Decisions

- `pipelines.rs` (288) holds the six constructors, the shared vertex layout, `FACE_DEPTH_BIAS`,
  `STENCIL_MASK`/`MAX_STENCIL_OWNERS` and the four depth-state builders. Its header states the
  depth, blend and stencil contract once instead of leaving it spread over the constructors.
- `gpu.rs` (97) holds the three wrappers, `line_list`, `triangle_list` and `clamp_size`. Its header
  says it owns device objects and no frame data.
- `lib.rs` keeps `MeshVertex`: its fields are built by the vertex builders, which are S17's move,
  so dragging it now would split one value family over two sessions. `pipelines.rs` and `gpu.rs`
  read it through `crate::MeshVertex` with `pub(crate)` fields, the way `scene`'s `ray.rs` reads
  `Ray` (S15, B8: a move rewrites no logic).
- `COLOR_FORMAT` and `DEPTH_FORMAT` stay in `lib.rs` as `pub(crate)`: `RenderTargets` creates the
  textures with them, so a reader finds the format where the texture is made, and the pipelines
  import it rather than restating it (C7).
- `GpuMesh` construction stays inline in `upload` with `pub(crate)` fields. A `GpuMesh::new` would
  have moved `upload`'s index-count logic into the move; S17 decides the upload shape when it takes
  the vertex builders.
- `stencil_slot`, `edge_geometry`, `EdgeSlice` and `sort_back_to_front` stay in `lib.rs`: they read
  a frame (`Drawable`, `EdgeSlice` slices per drawable), which is S17's subject.
- The tests did not move (T2 is optional and never mixed into a move): the module gained one `use`
  line for the four names that are now private to `pipelines.rs`.

## Ruled out

- Moving `MeshVertex` into `gpu.rs`: it is the vertex format two modules share and one module
  builds, and the builders leave in S17 — the move would be undone a session later.
- A `pipelines::SHADED` table of prebuilt pipeline pairs: the renderer needs the device and layout
  at construction time, and a table would be a second description of what `ViewportRenderer::new`
  already does in six lines.
- Splitting the test module into `pipelines_tests.rs` beside its new module: a test-layout change
  (T2) in a move commit, and it would hide which assertions the move disturbed.
- Reaching for `pub(super)` instead of `pub(crate)`: `mod pipelines` and `mod gpu` are children of
  the crate root, so the two are the same visibility; `pub(crate)` says it once.
