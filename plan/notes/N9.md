# N9 — a niche is a convex cell of wall planes, in the product frame

a niche is a convex cell of wall planes, in the product frame

## What was measured

- `Project::niche` (`crates/model/src/project/niche.rs:205`) casts four rays along the
  product's own axes, cuts each face plane with the section plane through the cursor's
  height (`wall`, `:357`), intersects the four lines pairwise (`section`, `:266`) and
  extrudes the polygon along `Z` from the two vertical probe distances (`:228-241`).
  It is exact only while every wall is vertical and the floor and ceiling are horizontal.
- A wall tilted across the section is cut at the cursor's height only: `wall` builds the line
  from `local.z`, and `covered` is sampled at `floor + COVER_INSET` and
  `ceiling - COVER_INSET` (`:322-330`), so a wall whose line moves with height either fails
  the coverage check or passes with a section that is wrong at every other height.
- Scratch test on this tree, removed afterwards: a back wall 720 mm tall leaned 15 degrees
  about world X, in a 600 x 560 x 720 product, answered `NicheRefusal::Unrepresentable` for
  a point at (300, 280) and z = 100, 360 and 600. The same tree with a ceiling leaned
  10 degrees answered `NicheShape::Box` with a height of 696.473, 650.628 and 604.783 mm for
  y = 20, 280 and 540; a shelf created there would cut into the ceiling at the back.
- A product turned 45 degrees about Z is refused with `NicheRefusal::Turned` (`:212`), although
  the section is already taken in the product's own frame (`local`, `inverse`) and the box
  answer is only used for `local_min`/`size`.
- `SurfaceBox::face_hit` (`surface.rs:88`) already answers the true face of a turned board:
  the distance, the world point, the free-side normal and the face axis. The cell needs no
  new surface geometry, only the plane of each face met.
- `crates/geom` has exact 2D regions (`boolean.rs`, `ring.rs`) and no 3D polytope; a cell is a
  new value in `model`, clipped in `f64` millimetres in the product frame and quantised once
  when it becomes a document ring.
- `Niche` is consumed by `apps/furnicraft/src/fill_gizmo.rs` only (`plan_at`, `shelf`,
  `post`, `refusal_sentence`); the move rule uses `SurfaceBox`, not the niche.

## Decisions

- The free space is the intersection of half-spaces: start from the envelope's six planes,
  probe from the point along every current face normal and every current vertex, plus the 26
  envelope directions in the first round, clip the cell by each met face's plane, and repeat
  until a round adds nothing. Cap the rounds and the face count and refuse when the cap is hit.
- The cell is kept in the product's frame; `local_min`/`size` stay its world box for the
  callouts, `min` its world corner, and `Turned` is deleted. The product frame is already the
  frame the probes are transformed into; the refusal was a leftover from the box-only query.
- Every face must be covered by a surface over its whole extent: sample the plane outward from
  just inside each face vertex and the face middle, the way `covered` does today, instead of
  the floor and ceiling only. This is the check that refuses a half-height board.
- No surface's box corner may lie strictly inside the cell. A void that holds a post or a
  shelf is not convex; the cell would include the obstacle and the part would cut into it.
  Refusing is honest until the query can return several cells.
- A non-convex void (a notch, two boards meeting in the middle) fails one of the two checks above
  and is refused, not approximated. A curved boundary is measured by the box around its body,
  which encloses it, so the cell is conservative: the part fits with a gap and never cuts in.
  An exact curved section needs a surface query that can meet a curved face; it is not here.
- The fill keeps fitting boards: the shelf's footprint is the cell's section clipped over its
  thickness (each wall half-plane evaluated at the far face, the stronger of the two ends),
  and the post's is the same in the panel's own plane (the product's ZY against the panel's
  local XY, the free axis along the product's X). The intersection is a convex polygon; a
  rectangle answer keeps the nominal panel, anything else is the contour N7 already creates.
- Tolerances: keep the micrometre quantisation of the probes, `COVER_INSET` (1 µm) for the
  coverage samples and `COORDINATE_TOLERANCE` (2 µm) for comparing a face with a cell vertex.
  The cell is clipped before quantisation, so its corners are not rounded twice.
- The cut API the fill needs is "the cell cut by a plane and projected on two product axes";
  both fills call it with the far face of the slab, which is the face that can violate a wall.

## Ruled out

- The owner's first-and-second hit along a fan of rays: the second hit is the back face of a
  board and adds nothing to the free boundary, and a polygon from sampled rays approximates the
  corners; the trapezoid's vertices must be exact (N7's note already ruled out the fan).
- Keeping the four-wall section and adding vertical scans: the result is still one extrusion
  along Z, so a wall tilted across the section cannot be represented at any height.
- A voxel or boolean model of the free space: it is a second representation to keep in step and
  it cannot place a corner exactly; the probe already reports the face plane.
- Splitting a non-convex void into several cells: deferred with the obstacle case; the first
  stage refuses, and the sentence says what to point at instead.
