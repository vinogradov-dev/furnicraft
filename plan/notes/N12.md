# N12 — the shaped fill puts the bevel on the wall and the wide face on B

the shaped fill puts the bevel on the wall and the wide face on B

## What was measured

- N9's section over the slab is the intersection of the wall half-spaces at the two faces, so it
  fits with vertical side faces and touches each wall at one face only. The owner wants the flush
  fit, which is exactly the bevel of N11.
- For a wall with the free-side unit normal `n` in the panel's frame, the face at `z = 0` (B)
  answers `n_xy . xy >= offset`, the face at `z = t` (A) answers
  `n_xy . xy >= offset - n_z t`. When `n_z < 0` the A edge is inset by
  `d = -n_z * t / |n_xy|`, the wide face is B and a positive bevel cuts A. When `n_z > 0` the
  wide face is A and a cut-only board cannot fill the void from a B outline.
- A wall with `|n_xy|` near zero is parallel to the board: a floor or ceiling crossing the slab.
  The section shrinks to nothing across the thickness, so a flat board cannot fill the void.
- `FillArm` (`crates/ui/src/editor/fill.rs:27`) carries the class and the fill pair; the shell's
  `plan_at` (`apps/furnicraft/src/fill_gizmo.rs:270`) is the only consumer of `Project::niche`
  and the only writer of the ghost's placement.
- ADR 0105: a stance is the flat frame turned and never mirrored; a panel turned on its own is not
  in a stance. The hand and the face labels are derived from where the board stands, so a board
  placed with its wide face up is a placement, not a fourth stance.

## Decisions

- Shape mode takes the section at the wide face B: build the cell's section at the B face (the
  union end), give every edge whose wall has `n_z != 0` a bevel of
  `beta = atan(n_z / |n_xy|)` with the sign that cuts face A, and leave a perpendicular wall at 0.
- If every wall's `n_z` in the panel's frame is not of the cutting sign, turn the board about its
  length axis so B is the wide face. A shelf whose wide face is its top is the flat stance turned
  half a turn about `X`; a post is the stance turned half a turn about its own length. The turn is
  a placement; the contour is mapped into the turned local frame before it is stored.
- If the walls disagree in sign, no flat board fills the void: refuse with a sentence. A wall
  nearly parallel to the board (a wedge void) is refused as well; Fit remains the safe answer.
- Only a bevel-free shape goes through N10's standard classifier. A beveled board is a contour
  with bevel records, whatever its polygon looks like, because the `Rect` generator has no place
  for an end bevel and N11 refuses a banded edge.
- `panel.create` gains an optional bevel list next to `outline`, so one click is still one
  command and one undo entry. The ghost draws the A rim from the beveled ring, not by lifting
  the B corners by the thickness.
- A new ADR records the reverse placement and the wedge refusal; ADR 0105 gains a note that a
  fill may turn the board about its length axis and why.

## Ruled out

- A growing bevel (a negative angle) for the widening case: the body would exceed the stored
  outline, and the nominal size, the core region and the cutout validation all assume it does not.
- Refusing the widening case: it is half of the real leaning cases, and a turn of the board
  costs one rotation and keeps the stance's length axis.
- Deriving the bevel from the wall in the shell: the model owns the measurement (ADR 0103), and a
  report or a CLI must be able to answer the same plan without the ghost.
- A second cut for the wedge void (a sloped top face): that is a variable-thickness panel, not an
  end bevel, and it is not this feature.
Anchor re-read: `FillArm` `editor/fill.rs:23`, `plan_at` `fill_gizmo.rs:268`. The written angle is `beta = atan(-n_z / abs(n_xy))` with the wall's free-side normal, positive for the cut at face A.
Owner left the reverse placement and the wedge refusal to the implementation on 2026-11-14 and asked that the four tasks stay queued behind K2–K4, not started.
## What the implementation measured

- The plan is the section at the wide face, cut with `Niche::cut(t, (face, face), (u, v))`; with every
  wall's `n_t = 0` the single-face cut and N10's slab cut are the same lines, so a plumb void answers
  exactly what it did before (verified by the existing trapezoid and box tests).
- Matching a section edge to the wall that bounded it: the plane's normal points into the void and so
  BACK at the board, so it equals the edge's OUTWARD normal turned around — the dot product is -1, not
  +1 — and its offset must be divided by the flat normal's length, because `cut_planes` keeps the
  unnormalised `flat · (u,v) >= offset - n_t * face`. Both mistakes made a wall silently ask for no
  bevel.
- A wall leaned by `delta` about a case axis narrows the board at face A when its face moves away from
  the void as the height rises; the sign of `n_t` in the board's frame says which, and the fill's
  placement only has to be re-derived when the sign says the wide face is A. To make a document fixture
  of the turning case the wall must stand INSIDE the case, otherwise it never bounds the section at the
  wide face and the plan comes back with no bevel at all.
- The bevel cap the fill applies is its own 45 degrees (`MAX_FILL_BEVEL`), not the schema's 89: past a
  mitre the end face is a taper across the large face, which is a variable-thickness panel. That is what
  refuses the 10-degree ceiling (an 80-degree end face) with the wedge sentence.
