# N14 — the niche is bounded by the faces the pointer sees

what the session measured and why the two refusals were wrong

## What was measured

- `apps/furnicraft/src/fill_gizmo.rs` mapped the pointer with `envelope_box`, the **world-axis box**
  around the product envelope's eight transformed corners, then `envelope_entry` intersected the ray
  with that box. A product turned 30 degrees answers `Outside` for every ray that enters the world
  box in a corner outside the rotated envelope; the owner saw "Point at the empty space inside the
  open product" while pointing at the piece.
- The model's `niche` refused a cell whose wall did not cover the whole face of the envelope
  (`covers_face`). A product of 1200 x 800 x 720 with a 1200 x 600 top and two 720 x 600 walls, i.e.
  an ordinary open front, refused every point at z = 300 with `Unrepresentable`. The same case with
  a top covering the whole envelope answered `Box`. So did an empty envelope with panels only at the
  back: the missing front margin was read as an uncovered wall.
- A turned wall pair that stops at its own edges answered `Unrepresentable` for the same reason; a
  splayed pair that crosses the whole depth answered `Section`.
- Shape mode refused `WallsDisagree` and `Wedge` when the wall planes could not all be met by one
  flat end face. Under a ceiling leaned 10 degrees the slab cut is a rectangle, so the refusal hid a
  perfectly good board.

## Decisions

- The measured point is the first face the view ray meets **inside the product's own envelope**, a
  micrometre on its free side; the envelope is tested in the product's frame, never by its world
  bounds. A ray that meets only the far envelope keeps the entry point (ADR 0109).
- The whole-face coverage test is deleted. A met face's plane bounds the cell wherever the face
  stands; `holds_body` remains the check for an obstacle standing inside the cell.
- Shape falls back to the slab cut with no bevels when `reference_face` answers `WallsDisagree` or
  `Wedge`, instead of refusing.
- Tried and ruled out: clipping the cell by each met face's own rectangle (the face's prism). A
  ceiling's side planes are slanted and cut the shelf's footprint with a plane that is not a wall at
  that height; a wall's end planes cut the niche at its open front. The test suite caught the leaned
  ceiling at y = 523.86 instead of 560, so the prism idea is not in the tree; the exact connected
  free region needs a slab cross-section that subtracts each surface's real footprint.
- The shell's cache keys on the measured point, not the revision: after a click the tool stays armed
  and the pointer's first face may be the part just created, so the post-click ghost is a second
  proposal, not the one the history recorded. The frame tests capture the ghost on the press frame.

## Left open

- A part can extend past a board's visible edge into open space inside the envelope. This is safe and
  fills the common case, but the exact niche outline needs the 2D slab region described above.
- The two public `FillRefusal` variants `WallsDisagree` and `Wedge` survive as the classifier's own
  refusals; `fill_plan` no longer returns them.

## Follow-up

- `a_splayed_case_under_a_short_top_offers_every_build` adds the owner's combined case — two boards
  turned in the product frame plus a top that stops before the open front — to the fill tests after
  the close. It plans both fills in both builds, because either refusal would reproduce the report.
