# N7 — the niche is a section, and the shelf takes it

## What was measured

- `SurfaceBox` (`crates/model/src/project/surface.rs`) kept only the world axis-aligned bounds of a
  surface, so `probe` stopped at the box around a turned panel. Two posts turned about the vertical
  axis would have answered as two world boxes: the section could never become a trapezoid.
- `Project::niche` (`crates/model/src/project/niche.rs`) cast six rays whose distances alone formed an
  axis-aligned box, and `NicheShape` had one variant, `Box`. `Probe` reported only `distance_mm` and
  `ProbeHit`; the face the ray met was thrown away.
- `FillGhost`/`plan` (`apps/furnicraft/src/fill_gizmo.rs`) built a box from the niche's size and the
  pointer's height, and `panel.create` could only make a nominal rectangle. `panel.contour.create`
  exists but takes its ring from a constructive object in the scene, which a ghost does not have.
- The niche's six probes are cast from the point the cursor ray leaves the envelope at
  (`envelope_entry`), which lies *on* the envelope's near face: a query that demands the point be
  strictly inside its walls refuses every real hover.
- The sample scene of the shell tests (`sample_project`) has a 600 mm tall board standing just outside
  the product's front face, flush with it: its plane is a wall over only 600 of the 720 mm void. A
  check "the surface that stopped the probe spans the whole height" refuses the frame test's own
  scene, although the envelope face behind the board is coplanar and does span it.
- `files:` named `crates/model/src/project/query.rs`, `crates/geom/src/lib.rs` and
  `apps/furnicraft/src/gizmo.rs`; the query split into `niche.rs` and `surface.rs` in N6, and the
  corner widget has nothing to do with a niche. The hint was corrected with
  `cargo xtask task set N7 --field files --files …`.

## Decisions

- **The section is measured in the product's own horizontal plane (X, Y) and extruded along its
  height.** A shelf is a horizontal board whose outline lies in that plane, so that is the plane whose
  free shape it takes. ADR 0099 and the entry say "vertical plane facing the viewer, extruded along
  the depth", which describes a prism whose section a shelf cannot take: a shelf's board would show
  that section edge-on. The 3D meaning — the free space is one prism — is kept, the axis named
  correctly, and `docs/domain/products.md` §4 now says so.
- `SurfaceBox` keeps its own frame (`local_min`, `local_max`, `world`) beside the world bounds. The
  move rule keeps reading the world bounds; `SurfaceBox::face_hit` takes the ray into the surface's
  own frame and answers the true face, its world point, its normal (always back along the ray) and
  its axis. Rotation keeps lengths, so the distance is the same in both frames.
- `Probe` carries `ProbeFace { point, normal }`. The point is built from the quantised distance, so a
  section corner built from two faces is consistent with the distances the niche reports.
- `NicheShape::Section(Contour)` stores the polygon as the document ring a panel would store, in the
  product frame. `geom::Region` validates the polygon first (`from_loops` refuses a degenerate or
  self-crossing section); the ring is built from the same corners, rounded to the micrometre, so the
  document owns the second representation rather than a geometry type at the ghost.
- `NicheRefusal { Outside, Turned, Unrepresentable }` replaces the bare `Option`: the tool can say
  why there is no ghost, which the entry's "readable refusal" asks for.
- **The walls, not the distances, bound the section.** Each side probe's face is cut with the section
  plane and gives a line with a unit normal pointing into the free space (a tilted face is handled by
  the plane's offset along z). The four lines meet pairwise into the corners `R∩B, B∩L, L∩F, F∩R`,
  which is counter-clockwise starting at the far-right corner. A corner must be on the free side of
  all four lines, the area must be positive, each edge must lie inside the face that stopped its probe
  (`within_face`, in the surface's own frame), and each wall's plane must still hold a surface at the
  void's floor and ceiling (`covered`). A corner may sit on a wall: the cursor does.
- `covered` probes outward from a point one micrometre *inside* the free space, because a ray that
  starts on a face and points into the solid answers the far face. It tests the **plane**, not the
  surface, so a board flush with an envelope face still counts as the wall the envelope continues.
- Tolerances: distances are quantised to the micrometre, so a corner where two walls meet can land a
  micrometre outside a face. `COORDINATE_TOLERANCE` (2 µm) covers the comparisons, `SECTION_TOLERANCE`
  (1 nm) the exact ones, `COVER_INSET` (1 µm) the sampling inset. `local_min`/`size` are the box
  around the section combined with the void's floor and ceiling, and the floor/ceiling themselves are
  quantised once (the cursor is not on a whole micrometre).
- A section whose corners equal the four probe distances is `NicheShape::Box`; a shelf keeps the
  rectangle panel it has always created, so the common case loses nothing (nominal-size fields,
  banding, resize).
- `panel.create` gained `outline: Option<Contour>`. A ring makes the part a contour panel in one
  command and one undo entry (`Project::add_panel_contour`), with the tool's class written by the same
  run; `nominal_size` must equal the ring's bounds, so the two parameters cannot disagree. No new
  command, no constructive-object detour.
- The ghost stores the anchored ring, the placement the command will store, the bottom and top rims in
  the world, and the panel's own X/Y axes for the callouts. It paints the rim, the far rim and one edge
  per corner, so a rectangle still draws twelve edges and a trapezoid reads as a trapezoid.
- A post is a flat board across the depth: a splayed section gives it no face to stand against, so the
  tool refuses it with a sentence rather than cutting the board into the walls.

## Ruled out

- Building the section as a fan of many rays (an approximated polygon): the vertices must be exact,
  and the two posts' trapezoid has four.
- Taking `Region` all the way to the ghost and converting to `Contour` in the shell: unit conversion
  belongs to the model, and the ghost would carry two shapes for one part.
- Making every shaped shelf a contour panel, rectangles included: a contour panel loses `panel.resize`
  and the rectangle card (ADR 0042), and the entry asks that a rectangular case still yields its
  rectangle.
- Requiring a wall's *surface* to span the void's height: the shell's own sample scene puts a board
  flush with the envelope face and stops 120 mm short, and the niche there is a perfectly good box.
- Allowing the post with a free range clamped to the section's inner width: the range is exact for a
  trapezoid, but the entry asks for the shelf, and the gap is filed in `plan/debt.md` instead of half
  a rule.

## Verification

Model: a probe meets the true face of a turned board where the world box would have reported a wall,
the six box cases are unchanged, two angled posts give the trapezoid `(400,560) (200,560) (100,0)
(500,0)` with its box `[100..500] × [0..560] × [0..720]`, a half-height wall is
`NicheRefusal::Unrepresentable`, a turned piece is `Turned` and a point outside is `Outside`. Engine:
`panel.create` with a ring stores a contour panel anchored at its zero corner with the tool's class,
and one undo removes it. Tool: the shaped shelf's anchored ring and world corners, the post's refusal,
a cursor on the envelope face, both offscreen paint checks (three callouts and at least twelve
edges). Shell: a headless frame creates the shaped shelf and proves the stored ring, placement and
size are exactly the ghost's. `cargo xtask ci` is green; the fill shot in
`target/ui-shots/fill-ghost.png` was not re-rendered (the shaped ghost is covered offscreen).
