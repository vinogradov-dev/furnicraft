# N10 — two fill modes and a standard panel whenever the section allows one

two fill modes and a standard panel whenever the section allows one

## What was measured

- `apps/furnicraft/src/fill_gizmo.rs` has one shape per fill: `shelf` (`:347`) takes
  `niche.section()` as a contour and otherwise the niche box; `post` (`:394`) refuses any
  section that is not a rectangle (`:401`). There is no mode, no choice and no classifier.
- `PanelOutline` (`crates/model/src/panel/mod.rs:44`) already carries a generated rectangle with
  `CornerFeatureKind::Radius { radius }` and `Chamfer { incoming, outgoing }`
  (`crates/model/src/panel_features.rs:16`), the second asymmetric, so a chamfered or rounded
  rectangle is document data today. `contour_with_sources_for` (`panel/contour.rs:601`) refuses a
  feature whose two setbacks leave a clear edge length not above 1e-6 mm (`:634`): a corner cut
  that consumes the whole edge (the N7 trapezoid's side) cannot be a rectangle feature.
- `Panel::new_contour` and `panel.create` with `outline` already create a contour panel in one
  command and one undo entry (`crates/engine/src/panel_cmds/panel.rs`), and `Panel::contour_size`
  derives the nominal size from the ring's bounds, so a contour needs no second size field.
- A contour panel loses `panel.resize`, the rectangle card and its nominal fields on purpose
  (ADR 0042), which is why the standard path must win whenever it is exact.
- The ribbon already draws a tool with several input methods as one split cell and a list
  (`crates/ui/src/toolbars/spec.rs` `VariantSpec`, U74); a fill cell is `ToolKind::Fill(ClassCode)`
  and carries no mode.

## Decisions

- Both modes start from the N9 section. Fit is the largest rectangle with sides on the panel's
  own axes inside the section, found from the convex polygon's breakpoints (a rectangle gives
  itself). Shape is the section itself. Neither needs a second measurement.
- The classifier is generate-and-compare, not a shape heuristic: build the nominal rectangle
  ring, then the ring for each set of corner features, and accept one only when it matches the
  section vertex by vertex within the micrometre. This is exact, it reuses the generator that
  will draw the panel, and a shape is never called standard on a guess.
- The trapezoid of two angled posts stays a contour: its sides are the whole nominal edges, so
  the clear length is zero and the generator refuses the chamfers. A rectangle with one cut
  corner, which does leave a clear edge, becomes `Rect` with one asymmetric chamfer.
- The mode is a method of the fill cell, not a new sticky setting: the primary method is Fit and
  the variant is Shape, and the pinned method the split cell runs is the session's choice, so
  nothing new is saved and the existing list already shows both names. `ToolAction::Fill`
  carries the mode into `FillArm`.
- Fit is the safe default: it always fits inside the slab and keeps the rectangle; Shape is the
  answer to "take the whole shape", and N12 makes it flush with bevels.

## Ruled out

- A sticky boolean in `Toolbars`: the ribbon's method list already expresses "the same tool with
  another input method", and a second mechanism would need its own settings key and its own
  drawing.
- A greedy rectangle (start at a corner, grow until a wall): it is not the largest one, and a
  trapezoid has a better rectangle that does not touch the corner.
- Classifying by angle thresholds ("all corners within 1 degree of 90"): it would call a
  nearly-rectangular section standard and store a ring that is not the section.
- Making every shaped fill a contour (N7's choice): the owner explicitly asks for the standard
  panel with chamfers or radii first, and a rectangle card keeps resize and banding.

What the next session would otherwise re-search:
- The classifier lives in crates/model/src/panel/classify.rs and its input is a *document* ring anchored at [0,0] (micrometres), not a polygon: it converts with `Contour::to_geometry` and compares geometry segments. Arcs are compared by centre, radius and both endpoints; a radius is derived from the corner's two setbacks and the centre at `corner - incoming*r + left*r` (a corner radius is NOT centred on the corner).
- `PanelOutline::ring_for` / `ring_and_sources_for` are the generator, lifted out of `Panel::contour_with_sources_for` into the free `outline_ring_with_sources`. Any new candidate family the classifier gains must go through them, or the classifier can accept a shape the panel would not draw.
- The largest inscribed rectangle is NOT a breakpoint enumeration: a right triangle or a diamond has its optimum with sides at no vertex x. Because `high` is concave and `low` convex on a convex section, the log area is concave in the two x sides, so a nested ternary partial maximisation is exact; the breakpoint pairs stay as the seed. `largest_rectangle` is private to project/fill.rs and tested there directly.
- A fill's free axis position and the parent-frame span come from the plan (`free_low`/`free_high`), so the ghost no longer computes them; `fill_gizmo::plan_at` only builds the world wireframe. `free_axis` now lives in the model (`furnicraft_model::free_axis`).
- The ribbon's split cell lost its method-label rebuild: `main_face` uses the row's own hint, and `method_rows` names the method for every tool except a fill, whose hint names the tool too («Post: Largest fit») because two cells would otherwise read the same. A disabled face now says what is missing instead of the method name — that is a behaviour change worth keeping.
- `PanelCreate::outline` is `Option<PanelOutline>` now; a contour is still anchored and size-checked as before, a Rect keeps its nominal size and its features are generated (and refused on overlap) before the panel enters the tree by `Project::add_panel_with_outline`.
