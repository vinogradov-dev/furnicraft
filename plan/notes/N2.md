# N2 — The product envelope is a dashed box, and it picks by its box.

The product envelope is a dashed box, and it picks by its box.

## What was measured

- ADR 0100 was written after the entry: it amends 0097 so the frame **is** picked, and it fixes the
  three states. The backlog title ("never picks") predates the amendment; the detail and the ADR
  win, so the first `files:` path was read with that in mind.
- `SceneFrame` carried drawables, line chains, shadows and the grid, and `pick` tested only solids
  and chains. `crates/scene/src/ray.rs` had a triangle and a segment cast but no box cast.
- `SceneColors` carried the background, the grid and the unlit line ink; the viewport had no ink for
  the envelope and `theme::scene_colors` is the one bridge from the active variant.
- The line pipeline already writes no depth (`line_depth_state`) and the renderer draws lines after
  the opaque faces, so a dashed buffer needs no new pipeline and no new depth state.
- `Session` has no open product: ADR 0100 and N3 put `active_product` in the session, and the sample
  scene has no product at all, so the open ink is unreachable from the shell until N3.
- The model refuses a product inside a product, so "nested" in the picking rule means one product
  standing in the void of another as a sibling (`docs/domain/products.md` §3).

## Decisions

- The scene item is `ProductEnvelope { id, size, world_transform, state }`: `frame` is banned in
  code by `docs/domain/products.md` §1, and the transform plus three sides is what both the twelve
  edges and the local-space pick need. `ProductEnvelopeState` is `Open`, `ClosedEmpty` or
  `ClosedWithParts`, and only the last one draws nothing.
- A closed product with parts is still pushed into the frame: it draws no lines but its interior is
  picked, which is the only way a finished piece can be selected (ADR 0100, V3).
- The two inks live in `SceneColors` (`product_envelope_active/inactive`) and follow the variant in
  `apply_colors`; the active ink is the palette's acting colour (copper) and the inactive one is a
  neutral grey, kept above the background in dark and below it in the light variant.
- The dash cycle is a twenty-fourth of the longest side with a 6 mm floor and a 55 % duty. A cycle
  in world space survives the camera moving without a re-upload, and tying it to the piece keeps a
  600 mm cabinet and a 3 m wardrobe about equally dashed at a fitted view.
- Picking: a solid or a chain always wins; only a click on empty space reaches an envelope. Among
  envelopes the smallest volume wins (the tightest box is the piece meant), and two equally tight
  boxes tie to the nearer entry, so the answer never depends on tree order. The ray is moved into
  the product frame and tested as a slab against `[0..size]`, with a micrometre of slack so a point
  that lies on a face stays inside after the inverse rotation.
- `Evaluation::collect_product` derives the state from `open_product` and from whether the subtree
  drew anything. The envelope joins `bounds.all` (an empty piece must frame and its box is visible)
  but never `bounds.solid` (a reserved box is not a body and casts no shadow).
- `evaluate` takes `open_product: Option<Id>`; `Document` passes `None` until N3 adds
  `Session::active_product`, so no session state was invented here.
- The renderer gets one more line buffer, uploaded from `product_envelope_vertices`, and draws it
  last with the existing line pipeline. An ignored GPU test writes
  `target/ui-shots/product-envelope-dark.png` and `-light.png` and asserts the active and inactive
  inks reach pixels while a closed-with-parts frame paints neither; both pictures were looked at.

## Ruled out

- A screen-space dash in a second fragment entry: geometry uploads only when the document moves, so
  the pattern would need a new shader and pipeline for a look the world-space cycle already gives,
  and the CPU dashes are tested without a device.
- A separate line pipeline or depth state for the envelope: the existing line state already writes
  no depth and compares `LessEqual`, which is exactly the promise.
- A selection tint on the envelope: the state owns the colour (ADR 0100), and the grips that show a
  selected frame are V3's.
- Storing the open state in the document: view state belongs to the session or the shell (ADR 0029,
  ADR 0098), and `product.create` opening a frame is a session operation (N3).
- Adding `Session::active_product` here: N3 owns the field, the double click and the card; this task
  only had to leave the one argument a caller fills.
- Picking by the nearest entry alone: a larger box in front would steal the click from a product
  standing in its void, which is the case the innermost rule exists for.
- Deriving the envelope from the children: it is authored (ADR 0097) and a child may poke out.
